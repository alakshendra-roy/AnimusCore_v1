# Animus Core — 5-Year Master Roadmap (2026–2031)

**Status:** Internal strategic planning document. Not client-facing, not for distribution.
**Author:** Solo founder + Claude (analysis pass), 2026-09-09.
**Method:** Every claim about current repo/business state below is drawn directly from the codebase and its own documentation, not restated marketing copy. Where a target figure (ARR, headcount, timing) is a goal rather than a measured fact, it is labeled **Target**, and where the underlying math is a stretch relative to what the business has actually proven so far, that gap is stated explicitly rather than smoothed over — the same "never fake it" discipline this codebase already applies to its own telemetry (`command-center.html` never simulates a number; this document extends that discipline to strategy).

---

## 0. Who this is for and how to use it

This is the operating plan for a single technical founder running Animus Core — a C++17 low-latency trading-infrastructure engine with a zero-dependency Python SDK — with no employees, co-founders, or contractors today, entirely self-funded. It assumes:

- **Automation replaces headcount wherever possible.** Every month below leans on tooling that already exists in this repo (CI gates, license generation, W-8BEN generation, benchmark verification, self-serve eval kits) rather than assuming a team to do things by hand.
- **Bootstrap ownership is retained through early pilot conversion.** Outside venture capital is an optional, downstream lever — not a dependency for Years 1–2 — evaluated explicitly at the Year 2/3 boundary below (§9.3), not assumed by default.
- **>85% gross margin is a hard constraint**, not an aspiration — it directly caps how much headcount Years 2–5 can responsibly add at each revenue level (§8).
- **Targets are targets.** Every ARR figure below is labeled and, where the math implies a specific deal mix or hire, that assumption is stated so it can be checked against reality every quarter, not just at year-end.

## 2. Operating principles that shape every month below

1. **Automation before headcount, always.** A new script or CI gate is the default answer to repeated manual work; a hire is the answer only when the work is fundamentally not automatable (legal counsel, direct enterprise sales relationships, hands-on colocation work).
2. **Sequence engineering by what actually blocks a deal, not by what's technically interesting.** Phase 14's tail-latency regression is prioritized in Month 1 because prospects will benchmark it themselves; Raft-lite durability is deferred to Year 2 because no tracked prospect has asked for cluster durability yet.
3. **Never let the roadmap's target ARR substitute for the deal-mix math that would actually produce it.** Every year section below states both the **Target** (what was set as the goal) and the **Base Case** (what the actual pricing tiers and realistic close rates imply), so a quarterly review can tell early whether the trajectory needs the optional funding lever pulled.
4. **Legal/compliance work is the one category that cannot be automated away.** Counsel review, incorporation, and tax-treaty compliance are on the critical path to *closing* revenue, not just building product — they get founder time budgeted every month in Year 1, not deferred to "later."

## 4. Year 2 (September 2027 – August 2028)

**Target: $10M+ ARR**, multi-exchange coverage, enterprise telemetry positioning established.

**Base-case reality check:** $10M ARR at a blended ~$150K–$250K/deal implies roughly 40–65 customers, or a much smaller number of Tier 4 global-strategic wins (e.g., 3–4 Tier 4 deals at $350K–$750K+ alone could be $1.5M–$3M, still leaving $7M+ to source elsewhere). Going from a Year 1 base of 5–10 customers to a $10M run rate in one year is a 6–15x jump — this is the point where solo-founder sales capacity is the real bottleneck, not engineering. **This is the most likely trigger point for the optional funding lever** (§9.3): either bring on the first hire dedicated to sales/BD, or accept a more conservative Year 2 ARR in the $2M–$5M range on pure organic conversion.

- **Engineering**: Ship CME MDP 3.0 and Eurex T7 adapters to the same CI-enforced, zero-heap-alloc, benchmark-gated standard as ITCH 5.0 — no adapter ships without the same rigor. Begin FPGA hardware-acceleration R&D (this is genuinely new technical ground, budget accordingly). Begin Raft-lite durable log storage / snapshotting now that a second engineer or dedicated sprint time can be justified by real ARR.
- **Commercial**: Convert Year 1 pilots into renewals and Tier 2/3 upsells; use the first 1–2 reference customers (finally available) to unlock the Tier 2/3 deals that were structurally unlikely in Year 1.
- **Team**: First hire — a contract or full-time C++ engineer to parallelize adapter work while the founder focuses on enterprise deal cycles and architecture. At 85% margin on even the base-case $2M–$5M, opex ceiling is $300K–$750K, which supports exactly one strong senior hire plus contractor budget, not more.
- **Legal**: Port `animus_verify_license` off Windows-only BCrypt so Enterprise licensing works on Linux builds — several Tier 2/3 prospects will run production on Linux.

## 5. Year 3 (September 2028 – August 2029)

**Target: $25M–$35M ARR**, execution gateways (OUCH, FIX 4.4) and colocation presence.

**Base-case reality check:** OUCH order-routing and FIX 4.4 execution gateways move Animus Core from a passive telemetry/ingestion vendor into the execution path itself — a materially higher trust bar (bugs here cost customers real money in flight, not just a missed metric) and a materially higher price point (this is exactly the kind of scope Tier 3/4 contracts are built for). $25M–$35M at a blended $300K–$500K/deal (justified once execution-path trust is established) implies roughly 60–115 customers or a denser mix of Tier 4 global-strategic accounts. Colocation deployments (global financial centers) introduce real, non-automatable operational cost and risk — this is not a feature to ship casually.

- **Engineering**: OUCH order-routing gateway, FIX 4.4 execution gateway — both need materially more rigorous testing/certification than a passive feed handler, given they sit in the execution path. Colocation deployment tooling (this is infrastructure/ops work, not pure software).
- **Team**: Lean scaling continues — likely 5–8 people total by end of Year 3 (engineering, one dedicated support/SRE role given execution-path SLA exposure, legal/compliance contractor engaged regularly rather than ad hoc). Still capped by the 85% margin rule: at $25M ARR, opex ceiling ≈ $3.75M.
- **Commercial**: Global financial-center colocation presence starts to matter for positioning against incumbent vendors; first real Tier 4 global-strategic conversations become credible once execution gateways exist.

## 7. Year 5 (September 2030 – August 2031)

**Target: $100M+ ARR**, global financial telemetry OS benchmark, IPO / strategic-acquisition readiness.

**Base-case reality check, stated plainly:** a solo-bootstrapped-start company reaching $100M ARR within 5 years would place Animus Core in the top tier of software company growth trajectories ever recorded — genuinely exceptional, not a default outcome of "keep executing the plan." At $100M ARR and the 85% margin target, opex ceiling is ~$15M, which at fully-loaded costs of ~$250K–$350K/person supports roughly **40–60 people** — a real company, no longer a "lean team" in the Year 1–2 sense, and one that almost certainly required the optional venture-acceleration lever (§9.3) to fund the sales, support, and colocation infrastructure a global Tier-1 client base actually requires, even with a highly automated core product. Treat $100M+ as the ambition that justifies every engineering and margin decision along the way, and treat the more literal Year 5 planning number as **$40M–$70M ARR on pure organic/bootstrap execution**, with $100M+ realistically requiring the funding lever to have been pulled by Year 2 or 3.

- **Engineering**: Global financial telemetry OS — the culmination of every adapter, gateway, and platform-maturity milestone above, positioned as category infrastructure rather than a point tool.
- **Commercial**: IPO or strategic-acquisition readiness workstreams begin (audited financials, formal board/governance structure — none of which exist today and all of which take real lead time to build).
- **Team**: 25–60 people depending on which funding path was taken, spanning engineering, sales, support/SRE, and legal/compliance — the point at which "solo founder" describes the company's origin, not its Year 5 operating reality.

## 9. Key risks and dependencies

### 9.1 Engineering risk
**Fixed 2026-09-09.** Phase 14's tail-latency regression directly affected the exact number a technical champion would benchmark first, which is why it was correctly Month 1's top priority. Root cause was pinning without priority elevation, not a hardware or OS-isolation limit — `pin_current_thread_to_core_exclusive` closes that gap, verified 15/15 runs improved (§1.1). Residual risk is smaller now, not zero: this is still thread-affinity-plus-priority, not full `isolcpus`/CPU Sets exclusive core reservation, so a sufficiently high-priority unrelated process could in principle still preempt it — worth knowing if a prospect's own hardware behaves differently during a real Proof-of-Performance.

### 9.2 Commercial risk
The 25-firm rigorous outreach sequence starting this week has **zero track record yet** — response rates, qualification-pass rates, and eval-to-paid conversion rates are all unknowns being estimated from the ≥3/5 framework, not from historical data. The first full cycle (Months 1–4) is the actual calibration data point this whole roadmap should be revised against.

### 9.3 The funding decision point
This roadmap explicitly retains 100% bootstrap ownership through early pilot conversion per the stated operating model, with institutional venture acceleration as an optional downstream lever — not a Year 1 assumption. Based on the base-case math above, **Year 2 is the most likely point where organic growth capacity (one founder's sales/delivery bandwidth) becomes the binding constraint rather than product or market demand.** The decision to raise capital, if made, should be evaluated against actual Year 1 close data (§3 footer), not against the stretch-case ARR targets — raising against a $350K–$650K real Year 1 outcome is a very different conversation than raising against $1.5M.

### 9.4 Legal/compliance risk
Every dollar of Year 1 revenue is gated on counsel review of the pilot contract templates (currently self-flagged as AI-drafted, tracked as GitHub issue #7) and a resolved liability-cap position. This is the one workstream with a hard external dependency (a real lawyer's calendar) that no amount of automation shortens — it is budgeted first in Month 1 for exactly that reason.
