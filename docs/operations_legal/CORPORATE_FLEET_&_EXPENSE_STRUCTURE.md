## 1. B2B Operating Lease / Corporate Fleet Subscription

### 1.1 The general model

A corporate operating lease / fleet subscription bundles depreciation/finance cost, insurance, scheduled maintenance, and (in some products) roadside assistance into one fixed monthly fee, invoiced to the Company, with no asset purchase and typically no residual-value risk retained by the lessee. Categories worth comparing against each other before picking one:

| Category | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Car-subscription platforms** (consumer-originated, corporate tier) | Myles Corporate / Smart Subscription | Myles' subscription tiers run 3–48 months, bundle insurance/maintenance/roadside assistance, and have OEM tie-ups (Maruti Suzuki, Toyota, MG) — confirm current corporate-account terms directly, since Myles' primary published positioning is individual/consumer subscription and corporate-desk terms may differ from the public consumer product. |
| **Dedicated corporate/business leasing companies** | Orix Lease Plus (Orix India), Avis Corporate Lease, and similar established fleet-leasing firms | These are structured specifically as B2B operating-lease products (vs. a consumer subscription product extended to corporates) — get a comparative quote alongside Myles before deciding, since the two categories price and structure differently. |
| **OEM dealer corporate/business desks** | A Mercedes-Benz India dealer's corporate sales desk | Most premium OEMs maintain a separate corporate/business channel (different pricing, priority allocation, sometimes a direct lease-financing tie-up) from the retail showroom — ask the specific dealer whether their corporate desk offers an operating lease directly or only facilitates financing/an outsourced lease partner. |
| **Unconfirmed** | "KTC" | Named in the source request for this document; not independently verified in this drafting pass. Get the exact legal entity name and offering before including it in any real comparison. |

### 1.2 Accounting treatment note — confirm which standard applies

A **genuine operating lease** (no ownership transfer, lessor retains residual risk) is a straight P&L expense under the older AS-19 lease-accounting framework. If the Company is, or becomes, required to prepare accounts under **Ind AS** (typically triggered by net worth/turnover/listing thresholds, not by every private company), **Ind AS 116** instead requires most leases — including what commercial parties call an "operating lease" — to be capitalized on the balance sheet as a right-of-use asset with a matching lease liability, subject to a short-term (≤12 months) or low-value-asset exemption. For a small, newly incorporated OPC/Private Limited company, Ind AS 116 likely does not yet apply — but this is exactly the kind of threshold that changes as the Company grows, so have the CA confirm which framework applies **before** assuming "operating lease = pure P&L expense, nothing on the balance sheet" as a permanent fact rather than a currently-true one.

## 2. Tax Breakdown: Company-Level OPEX Deduction vs. Director-Level Perquisite

These are **two separate, both-real tax events on the same lease** — not alternative ways of characterizing one event, and not a choice between them. Getting only one side right and ignoring the other is the actual compliance risk here, not either side individually.

### 2.1 Company-level: lease rental as a business expense

The monthly lease/subscription fee, invoiced to the Company, is deductible as a business expense under the "wholly and exclusively for the purposes of the business" principle (the long-standing test under the old Act's §37(1) — confirm the 2025-Act equivalent section with the CA per the banner above) **provided the car is genuinely a Company asset made available for the Director's use in connection with Company business** — the common and generally accepted structure for a director's company car. This is the "cost to company" side: the Company can deduct the full lease rental as OPEX; it does not need to apportion the deduction between "business %" and "personal %" itself, **because the personal-use element is instead brought to tax on the Director's side** (§2.2) rather than disallowed on the Company's side. Trying to get both — a full company-side deduction *and* no perquisite charge on the Director's payslip for a car the Director also uses personally — is the actual compliance failure mode to avoid, not a corner case to plan around.

Fuel and maintenance paid via the Company's own corporate fuel card / IOCL XTRAPOWER account (§3) are Company expenses on the same OPEX basis, and specifically move the perquisite calculation into the "expenses met by employer" column of the table in §2.2 (as opposed to the lower-value column that applies when the employee bears running costs personally) — see §2.2's note on this.

### 2.2 Director-level: Rule 3(2) motor-car perquisite — 2026 rates

Where a Company-owned or Company-leased car is used by a Director/employee **partly for official and partly for personal purposes**, and running/maintenance expenses are met by the Company, Rule 3(2) of the Income-tax Rules adds a **fixed monthly perquisite value** to the Director's taxable salary — this is the mechanism, not a workaround, and it applies regardless of whether the Company or the Director "wants" it to. The **CBDT notified revised rates on 20 March 2026, effective 1 April 2026** (Income-tax Rules, 2026), which superseded the far lower pre-2026 table:

| Engine (cubic capacity) | Expenses met by employer (Company pays fuel/maintenance directly, e.g. via XTRAPOWER §3) | + Chauffeur provided |
|---|---|---|
| **≤ 1.6 litres** | ₹5,000/month | + ₹3,000/month |
| **> 1.6 litres** | ₹7,000/month | + ₹3,000/month |

Most Mercedes-Benz passenger models exceed 1.6L displacement, so the **> 1.6L row (₹7,000/month, + ₹3,000/month if a chauffeur is provided) is the applicable figure** for a Mercedes lease in this structure — **confirm the exact figure with the CA against the finalized (not draft) Income-tax Rules, 2026 text in force for the assessment year actually being filed**, since this document's search-pass sourcing (March 2026 CBDT notification coverage) is not a substitute for reading the notified rule itself. This perquisite value is added to the Director's gross salary and taxed at the Director's own slab rate — it is a real, comparatively modest addition to taxable income (a few tens of thousands of rupees a year), not the full value of the lease or the car.

**If instead the Director bears running/maintenance costs personally** (no corporate fuel card, no company-paid maintenance), Rule 3(2) applies a different, lower valuation — get the current "expenses met by employee" column from the CA rather than assuming a number here, since this document has only verified the "employer meets expenses" column above (the one relevant if §3's XTRAPOWER account is actually used).

### 2.3 What this playbook does NOT attempt to settle

- The exact 2025-Act section number replacing old §37(1) (business-expense deductibility) and old §17(2) (perquisite definition) — confirm both with the CA.
- Whether 100% of a specific lease's monthly cost is defensible as OPEX if the car's actual usage pattern is predominantly personal rather than business — the "wholly and exclusively for business" test is a facts-and-circumstances standard, not a bright line this document can apply on the Company's behalf.
- GST input tax credit (ITC) eligibility on the lease rental and fuel — motor vehicle ITC has its own restrictions under GST law (generally blocked for passenger vehicles seating ≤13 unless used for specified purposes like further supply, transport of passengers, or driving training) that are independent of the income-tax treatment above; get a separate, specific answer on this before assuming ITC is available.

## 3. IOCL XTRAPOWER Corporate Fleet Account & Premium Fuel

### 3.1 XP95 vs. XP100 — both are real, distinct IOCL products

**Correction to this document's own 2026-09-24 draft:** an earlier version of this section incorrectly claimed IOCL had no "XP95" product — that was wrong, based on a search pass that only checked for XP100 and treated its absence from those results as XP95 not existing. A direct search confirms **both are real, separately branded IndianOil fuels**:

| Product | RON (octane) | Launched | Positioning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **XP95** | 95 | May 2021 | IndianOil's "first 95-octane petrol" — a premium step up from standard ~91 RON branded petrol, priced only modestly above regular premium fuel (a small per-litre markup, not a specialist/niche price point), positioned for everyday premium use, not exclusively performance cars. |
| **XP100** | 100 | 2020 | IndianOil's "first 100-octane petrol" — marketed specifically at high-performance vehicles (Porsche/Ferrari/Lamborghini/BMW M/Mercedes-AMG-class engines), priced well above XP95 and standard fuel, with narrower outlet availability. |

For a Mercedes-Benz lease under this playbook, **which grade is actually appropriate depends on the specific engine/trim** (a mainstream Mercedes-Benz saloon typically runs fine on premium ~95 RON fuel; only AMG-tier performance engines are the segment XP100 is marketed at) — confirm the manufacturer's actual minimum/recommended octane rating for the specific model leased before defaulting to the more expensive XP100 grade on the assumption that "higher octane is always better" for that engine. **Shell V-Power** (Shell's own premium-fuel line) is correctly named in the source brief and needs no correction.

### 3.2 XTRAPOWER account setup

IOCL's XTRAPOWER Fleet Card is a smart-card-based fleet-management and cashless-fuel-purchase program for corporates, with prepaid and credit facility options, multi-channel recharge (RTGS/NEFT/IMPS/UPI/cards/net banking), and built-in spend analytics/reporting. Setup steps:

1. Register as a corporate account via the XTRAPOWER portal/app, providing the Company's PAN, GST registration, and bank details.
2. Confirm eligibility and minimum-account terms for a single-vehicle corporate account directly with IOCL/XTRAPOWER — this document could not independently confirm whether there is a practical minimum fleet size for a corporate (vs. individual/retail) account; the program is publicly positioned for "fleet operators and corporates" broadly, but a one-car account's exact terms should be confirmed before assuming parity with a multi-vehicle fleet account.
3. Load the account via the recharge channels above; card transactions post directly against the corporate account for reconciliation.
4. Route resulting fuel spend into `/Car_Lease_Fuel/` per [`CA_MONTHLY_ACCOUNTING_SOP.md`](CA_MONTHLY_ACCOUNTING_SOP.md) — this is also the paper trail that supports "expenses met by employer" in §2.2's perquisite table, so keep monthly XTRAPOWER statements even though the perquisite itself is a fixed monthly figure, not tied to actual litres purchased.

### 3.3 High-octane fuel at non-XTRAPOWER outlets

XP95 has materially wider outlet coverage than XP100 (a mainstream premium-fuel product vs. a narrower performance-fuel rollout) — Shell V-Power sits closer to XP95 on availability than to XP100. Confirm actual outlet coverage for whichever grade the leased vehicle needs near the Company's actual operating area before assuming the corporate card resolves fueling logistics end-to-end, particularly if XP100 turns out to be the correct grade for the specific engine.
